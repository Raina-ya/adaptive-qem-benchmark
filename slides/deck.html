When Does Quantum Error Mitigation Pay Off Under a Fixed Shot Budget?

Accounting, decision reliability, and operational boundaries of readout correction and zero-noise extrapolation, studied in noisy classical simulation.

Zhen-Jun Liu
November 2026 · github.com/Raina-ya/adaptive-qem-benchmark
All results simulated. No hardware result is claimed.
The question

A better estimate is not the goal. A better decision is.

Error mitigation removes bias but spends extra circuits and inflates variance. Most benchmarks score the mitigated value. In practice the value feeds a choice: which candidate, which parameter, which circuit is best.

  • Q1. When does mitigation improve estimation at a fixed total budget?
  • Q2. When does better estimation produce a better decision?
  • Q3. Can runtime evidence tell when to trust mitigation, an extrapolation, or a certificate?
  • Q4. Where do readout correction, calibration reuse, and adaptive selection stop working?
Thesis: under a fixed budget, QEM improves expectation RMSE but does not guarantee decision reliability. Whether it pays off depends on error structure, signal gap, calibration cost, variance amplification, and model mismatch.

When Does QEM Pay Off?10
Finding 2 · mechanism

The decision depends on the decisive gap, and ZNE inflates its spread

  • Energy MSE splits into a common-mode term and a contrast term. Selection depends only on contrasts.
  • On the medium/dense graph, 77% of the MSE that ZNE removes comes from one control candidate that never contends for the top.
  • The top-two gap: ZNE shrinks its mean from 0.045 to 0.016 and widens its spread from 0.053 to 0.137.
  • The normal approximation predicts inversion rates of 0.20 and 0.45; observed 0.18 and 0.50.
Rejected explanation

“Raw rankings are stable because raw errors are shared.”

Off-diagonal error correlations across repeats are near zero for both raw and ZNE. The inversion comes from the gap’s variance, not from shared raw error.

Finding 3 · runtime tools

Gates, selectors, and certificates control noise, not bias

ZNE validity gate v2

False rejection 0.000, false acceptance 0.176

Chosen from 256 runtime-only candidates and scored once on 30 held-out circuits. Its slope check measures whether decay is resolved, not whether the extrapolate is right, so false acceptance rises with shots.

Selector v3 · no route passed

Regret 0.0031, the same as always-readout

At 1.63× the two-qubit gate executions and 0.64 coverage. Always-raw had the lowest regret (0.0024): candidates share one readout, so its bias cancels in the comparison.

Certification fails on near ties

35.9% of certified near-tie runs are wrong

Corrected looks keep false certification at 1.7–2.7% at zero margin, against 22.4–24.9% for peeking. But a biased raw estimator certifies the wrong winner in 100% of repeats after about 2,000 shots.

Every interval-based tool controls the σ term in Perr ≈ Φ(−(Δ+β)√N/σ). None of them sees β.
When Does QEM Pay Off?14
Finding 3 · readout at scale

A mismodeled readout correction does worse than none

Resource failure. Full-matrix correction fails in 93–100% of width-8 runs under a shared 4,096-shot budget; with enough calibration it completes, using 80–98.5% of all shots.

Model failure. Under pair-correlated readout, tensor-product, subspace, and matrix-free corrections become worse than raw. Pairwise-local correction stays below 0.05.

Blind warning. A 1,024-shot check warns in time in 76.2% of mismatch cases, but not when flips become correlated with equal marginals. Condition number never warns.

Mitigation pays off when it removes a bias that differs between the alternatives and outgrows its own variance cost.

Limits and next steps

What this does not show, and what comes next

Limits

Existence, not a law

Synthetic noise only. Three curated QAOA graphs, 30 holdout circuits, 20 selector tasks. Only four method families. Sequence of studies chosen adaptively; only the confirmatory set is confirmatory at project level.

Next

Detect differential bias

Extrapolate the pairwise gap directly, pair each candidate with a Clifford twin of known value, and test randomized compiling against coherent over-rotation.

Then

Predict before measuring

Register Proposition 1 predictions on 50+ new graphs before running them, add device-informed noise models, and use the open-plan budget for one readout-correlation experiment.

Judge a mitigation method by the decision it serves, not only by the error of the value it returns.
